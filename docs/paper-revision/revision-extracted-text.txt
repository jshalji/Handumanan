CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of the Study
Metro Cebu is rich in historical events that have shaped the city's identity. From its religious roots, from the first Spaniards who introduced Christianity, to the way Cebuanos live their day-to-day lives. These significant events have been commemorated through heritage sites such as churches, museums, monuments, and old structures. These sites play a crucial role not just in boosting tourism but, most notably, in the cultivation and preservation of our culture. Hence, these must be taken good care of and valued significantly.
Information about these cultural heritage sites can be easily accessed with a simple internet search, whether through a social media post, blog, website, or any digital platform. Thus, information systems enhance tourism promotion by allowing users to access platforms anytime, anywhere [5]. As studies show, when tourism information is well organized, visitors may have a positive experience and are more likely to visit additional places [1]. Hence, the Department of Tourism encourages the use of digital platforms to promote and raise public awareness of tourist spots [3]. 
 However, major concerns arise about the completeness, consistency,  and reliability of such information. Some details are vague or simply incorrect, reflecting personal interpretations of the cultural event and leading to misinformation. These lapses may result in both tourists and residents being misled or may not be fully aware of these heritage sites.
Another issue with such digital platforms is the lack of features that help individuals reach their destinations more quickly and conveniently, making it difficult for them to plan their visits. That said, platforms should be easy to use, with search functions and location-based services, as these are more user-friendly [6].
To address these concerns, the researchers propose Handumanan: A Web-Based Cultural Heritage Site Information System for Metro Cebu, which aims to create a platform where users can easily access organized information about cultural heritage sites located particularly in the cities of Cebu, Lapu-lapu, Mandaue, and Talisay, and explore these sites more conveniently.
1.2 Statement of the Problem
Information about cultural heritage on the internet is often incomplete, inconsistent, and unreliable. This may mislead individuals who are not fully aware of such heritage, leading them to fail to explore it fully.  Also, digital platforms lack features that suggest places to visit, making it harder for individuals to explore nearby locations or plan their visits.
To address these concerns, this study seeks to answer the following questions:
How can information on cultural heritage sites across Metro Cebu be effectively compiled and organized in a web-based system?
What are the key features of the system that enable users to access the site effortlessly?
How do suggestive features like location-based recommendations and itinerary planning contribute to the overall travel experience?
1.3 Objectives of the Study
1.3.1 General Objective:
To develop a web-based cultural heritage site information system for Metro Cebu that provides organized and accessible information for users.
1.3.2 Specific Objectives:
To develop a unified system to organize and store cultural heritage site information in Metro Cebu.
To design a system that allows users to search, browse, and view detailed information about heritage sites.
To integrate location-based features, an AI-powered itinerary planner, and a chatbot that assists users by recommending nearby heritage sites, generating travel plans, and answering user inquiries related to cultural heritage sites in Metro Cebu. 
1.4 Significance of the Study
This study is significant because it contributes to the development of a cultural heritage platform. Specifically, this study will be beneficial to the following:
Tourists. The system helps tourists easily find and explore heritage sites without relying on scattered sources. The clear, well-organized information provided by the system will improve the overall travel experience.
Tourism Offices and Local Government Units (LGUs). The system can help the authorities in promoting Metro Cebu’s heritage sites by providing a platform that boosts cultural heritage awareness.
Community. Proper implementation of the system and effective management of the heritage sites boost tourism, thereby creating job opportunities and contributing to community improvement.
Future Researchers. The study may serve as a reference for future projects related to tourism and information systems. This can also serve as a guide for developing improved or more advanced systems in the future.
1.5 Scope and Limitations
This study focuses on developing a web-based cultural heritage site information system for Metro Cebu. It only covers heritage sites located in Cebu City, Lapu-Lapu City, Mandaue City, and Talisay City. The system provides important details about each heritage site, such as its historical background, images, categories, and location. Its main features include search and browsing, location-based suggestions, a chatbot, and an AI-powered itinerary planner that recommends nearby heritage sites and suggested travel routes. The system can be accessed through web browsers using mobile phones and computers.
However, the study also has limitations. The system cannot be used without an internet connection. The accuracy of the information depends on the sources gathered during data collection, so some details may still be updated after testing and review. The system is also limited to a web-based platform and does not include a separate mobile application. Some features, such as map directions, route suggestions, and other online services, may not work properly if the internet connection is slow or if third-party services are unavailable. Also, the chatbot feature only answers questions about the history of the sites, compares the places, suggests nearby stops, and builds routes when explicitly asked for a trip plan. Furthermore, the website can’t determine road traffic conditions. Lastly, the website doesn’t have an analytics dashboard for admins to monitor visitor engagement or manage the crowd. 
1.6 Definition of Terms
To better understand the study, the following terms are defined based on how they are applied in this study:
Artificial Intelligence (AI). A system feature used to generate travel recommendations, suggest nearby heritage sites, and assist users in planning itineraries based on available site information and user preferences.
Chatbot. A built-in conversational assistance feature that helps users ask questions and receive information related to cultural heritage sites, directions, and travel recommendations within Metro Cebu.
Cultural Heritage Sites. These are locations in Metro Cebu with historical representation and cultural value, such as churches, museums, monuments, and old structures.
Database. An organized storage of system information. In Handumanan, Firebase Firestore stores user-related records, reviews, saved itineraries, favorites, and admin-managed heritage site records, and also uses built-in heritage site data. 
Feedback. A user review or comment submitted on a heritage site page to share ratings and opinions about the site. 
Firebase. A cloud-based platform used for authentication, database storage, and real-time data management in the system.
Geolocation. A function that detects the user's current location and is used to show nearby heritage sites.
Information System. A system that collects, stores, and shows information to users in an organized way.
Itinerary. A planned list of places to visit, arranged in a proper order based on travel time and the time needed for each place.
Leaflet Map Integration. The use of Leaflet map services to display the location of each heritage site and help users view nearby sites and directions. 
Location-Based Recommendation. A system feature that suggests nearby heritage sites based on the user’s current geographic location.
Local Government Unit (LGU). These are the local authorities who are responsible for maintaining the inventory of heritage properties, preserving them, and implementing cultural programs within Metro Cebu. 
Navigation System. A feature that provides route guidance and directions from the user’s current location to selected cultural heritage sites using interactive maps and geolocation services.
OpenStreetMap. An open-source digital mapping platform used by the system to display maps, heritage site locations, and navigation routes.
Responsive Design. A design approach that allows the system interface to automatically adjust and display properly across different screen sizes and devices.
System administrator. The person with admin access who is responsible for managing heritage site records in the system, including adding, updating, hiding, or deleting site information, and managing image URLs. 
Tourism. This is an activity in which individuals visit places to explore, enjoy, and learn about other places’ tangible assets (e.g., tourist spots) and intangible assets such as culture and identity.
User Account. A registered account handled through Firebase Authentication that allows users to access features such as reviews, favorites, and saved itineraries. 
Web-Based System. A type of system that can be used through a web browser with an internet connection.

2.1 Related Literature
Cultural Heritage and Tourism
To understand how people are shaped today, it is important to remember history. One way to remember it is to preserve cultural heritage. Cultural heritage helps protect the identity, traditions, and historical experiences of a community. It also supports tourism because heritage sites allow visitors to learn about the past and experience local culture. According to Timothy and Boyd, heritage tourism helps people understand traditions and historical backgrounds through actual places and cultural experiences [1]. Cultural heritage tourism also helps strengthen cultural awareness and local identity [13]. Heritage sites help people remember important events, traditions, and ways of life from the past. By preserving and promoting these places through digital systems and tourism, younger people can learn more about local history and why it should be protected [1], [9], [13]. In addition, heritage tourism encourages cultural appreciation among both residents and visitors by allowing them to experience historical sites firsthand rather than only learning from books or media. To promote heritage tourism and encourage more visitors to explore cultural destinations, heritage information should be well organized. Hence, digital tools are important in preserving and promoting cultural heritage, making it more accessible to a wider audience [9]. 
Digital Platforms in Tourism
In recent years, the tourism sector has changed because of technology. Many tourism businesses and organizations now use digital platforms to promote destinations and make tourism information easier to access. These platforms help users find details about tourist spots, maps, recommendations, and other travel-related information in one place. Because of this, users do not need to rely on many separate sources just to plan their visit. Online platforms also help increase visitor engagement by allowing tourism information to be shared and accessed more easily [7], [11]. Some tourism systems also use digital tools such as chatbots and recommendation features to assist users and improve their experience [21]. With the help of these systems, tourism services become more organized and convenient for both local and foreign visitors. Digital platforms also support tourism promotion by helping destinations become more visible to the public [30], [33]. In the Philippines, digital platforms are encouraged to improve accessibility and provide updated tourism content [3].
Web-Based Information Systems
An information system is used to collect, store, manage, and show data in an organized way. Web-based information systems are useful because users can open them through devices with an internet connection. In tourism and cultural heritage, this kind of system can help store important details such as site names, descriptions, locations, images, and visitor information. It also allows users to access tourism-related information whenever they need it [14]. These systems help manage organized data and support the development of useful platforms [15]. They also make it easier to store, find, and present information for users [16]. 
Usability and System Design
Usability is important in a web-based system because it affects how people use the platform. A system should have a clear layout, readable information, and proper navigation so users can find what they need without difficulty. Nielsen states that a system should be understandable and usable for its intended users [4]. Organized information also helps keep users interested while using the system [8]. A good interface can improve user interaction and satisfaction, especially when users need quick access to information [18]. In tourism systems, this is important because users often look for site details, maps, directions, and recommendations while planning their visit. For this reason, Handumanan should be designed to work properly on both mobile phones and computers [27]. 
Location-Based Services
With location-based services, users can find places based on their current location. Geolocation technologies help improve navigation and support better decision-making in digital systems [19]. Geographic information systems also help organize and present maps, locations, and spatial data [20]. Studies also show that combining maps with location-based features helps users find nearby attractions and plan their visits more easily [6]. Location-based services are useful in tourism because they help visitors discover nearby places and view possible routes. For Handumanan, these features help users find nearby cultural heritage sites and view directions through map services. 
Artificial Intelligence in Tourism
Artificial Intelligence is now used in tourism systems to provide suggestions and support user assistance. Smart tourism studies show that technology can improve tourism experiences by making information more personalized and accessible [21]. AI can help users by suggesting destinations, answering common questions, and supporting travel planning. In tourism platforms, chatbot features are useful because users can ask questions and receive quick responses. Technology also affects how users choose destinations and plan their trips [23]. For Handumanan, AI is applied through the chatbot and AI-powered itinerary planner, which help users ask questions and create suggested travel plans based on their location, time, and preferences. 
Web Technologies and System Development
Modern web technologies play an important role in developing interactive systems. These technologies help developers create websites that can display information, handle user actions, and connect to databases and other services. Web development tools support the creation of dynamic and responsive platforms that can run through web browsers [39]. Cloud-based services are also useful because they support online data storage and user authentication. Firebase provides tools for web application development, including authentication and database functions [29]. Mapping and routing tools are also important in Handumanan because the system includes location-based features. Leaflet supports interactive maps, while OpenRouteService provides route-related services [37], [38]. These technologies support the development of Handumanan as a web-based cultural heritage information system.

2.3 Synthesis of the Reviewed Literature and Studies

Technology also helps tourism systems become more useful by allowing users to find information, view destinations, and plan visits more conveniently [3], [9]. However, many existing platforms still have scattered or limited information, which makes it difficult for users to fully explore cultural heritage sites. 2.4 Research Gap
Even though many tourism platforms already exist, there is still no system that focuses on organizing cultural heritage information in Metro Cebu. Most platforms only provide general tourism details, so users still need to search from different sources to get complete information [6], [8]. Because of this, users may find it difficult to look for nearby heritage sites or plan their visits properly. Some heritage sites may also receive less attention because their information is not properly presented or promoted [3], [11].
This shows the need for a web-based system that brings together organized heritage site information, location-based features, chatbot assistance, and itinerary planning in one platform. Handumanan aims to address this gap by focusing on cultural heritage sites in Metro Cebu instead of general tourist destinations. Through this system, users can access heritage information more conveniently, become more aware of local culture, and explore heritage sites in the region more effectively.

METHODOLOGY
This chapter presents how the system was designed, developed, and tested for the study Handumanan: A Web-Based Cultural Heritage Site Information System for Metro Cebu. It includes the conceptual framework based on the Input-Process-Output (IPO) model, the research design, the system development process, the participants involved, and the methods used to collect and analyze data.
3.1 Conceptual Framework
The conceptual framework for Handumanan uses the Input-Process-Output (IPO) model. This type of model explains how the system works, detailing what information is fed into it, how it operates on that information, and what outputs result. It also provides insight into the user's interaction with the system and the flow of information in and out of the system.

3.1.2 Explanation of the Conceptual Framework
The IPO model serves as the foundation of how the Handumanan system operates:
Input: This is where users and admins enter all data into the system. Users put in their search queries, current location, preferences, questions for the chatbot, comments, ratings, etc. The system can also store information about heritage sites, including their names, descriptions, categories, images, locations, and visitor information. This information is added, updated, and managed by administrators to ensure that the system remains accurate and organized. 
Process: This stage demonstrates how the collected data is used. The system first organizes and stores the data in a Firebase database. It can then handle user operations such as searching, browsing, and filtering heritage sites. Location-based features recognize local locations, and an AI feature suggests locations and offers recommended tours. The system is also interfaced with map services to give directions and routes. User feedback is also collected and archived to aid system improvement. 
Output: This stage displays the system's output. Users can browse heritage sites, view detailed information, explore available suggestions, generate itineraries, and receive directions on maps. They may also inquire via the chatbot and leave comments after using the system. A dashboard provides administrators with management information and feedback from the site. These outputs enhance the user experience and promote heritage sites in Metro Cebu.  
3.2 Research Design
A Developmental Research Design is used in this study. This design was considered because the study's primary objective is to design and test a web-based system. For Handumanan, the researchers are not just identifying a problem; they also propose a solution through a cultural heritage site information system for Metro Cebu. 
3.3 Data Gathering Procedures
Data collection was conducted to identify the pain points of current methods for accessing information about cultural heritage sites. This involved: 
Review of Related Literature and Studies – The researchers reviewed books, journals, government reports, and online articles related to cultural heritage tourism and web-based systems. These were useful in pinpointing ideas and features that might be incorporated into the system.  
Online Research and Site Validation – The researchers gathered information from reliable online sources, including government sites, tourism pages, and trusted blogs for the heritage sites in Metro Cebu. Where possible, selected heritage sites were visited to validate their existence, collect images, and verify information.  
Interviews and Surveys – Informal interviews with the tourism officers, local residents, and possible users (students, tourists) were conducted. These assisted the researchers in gaining insight into their needs and the issues that they encounter when searching for information about heritage sites. A brief online survey was also distributed to gather user opinions on features they found useful, such as search tools, maps, itinerary planning, and the chatbot. 
Observation – Observation was used to see how people usually search for heritage information on websites and social media. This helped the researchers notice common problems, such as outdated information and the lack of location-based features. 
3.3.1 Ethical Considerations
In compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173), the researchers will adhere to ethical practices when developing and testing the Handumanan system. Since the system involves user accounts, location-based features, feedback, and information about cultural heritage sites, the researchers will make sure that the rights and privacy of all participants and users are protected.
Informed Consent
Before the participants use the system or answer the questionnaire, the researchers will explain what the study is about. They will be told that joining the study is their choice and that their answers will only be used for school/research purposes. The researchers will also inform them about the data that may be collected, such as their ratings, comments, and basic account information if they register in the system.
User Privacy and Data Protection
Any information received by the system will be collected only when required for its functions, namely user account details, feedback, saved itineraries, and location input (only in case the location-based feature is used). The information will not be shared with anyone else or used for anything other than the study. To ensure data security, only authorized users will have access to the admin machine. 
Location Data Privacy
Handumanan also comes with location-oriented capabilities: Users will be warned that their location data could be processed to recommend nearby heritage sites and generate travel routes. Only the user's location will be used for this purpose. Users can grant or deny permission for location access based on their preference. 
Accuracy of Cultural Heritage Information
The researchers will ensure that information about the cultural heritage site added to the system is sourced from trusted sources such as government websites and tourist offices. This is critical to not provide false or misleading information. The researchers will carefully examine the information before adding it to the system. 
Proper Use of Images and Content
Images and content for the system will be sourced from existing sources or, where possible, collected by the researchers. The researchers will not use any copyrighted materials without permission or citation. All information related to the heritage sites will also be presented well, as these are now part of Metro Cebu's history and culture. 
Confidentiality of Feedback and Survey Responses
The answers from surveys, interviews, and online feedback forms will be kept private. The names and personal information of the respondents will not be shown in the final paper unless they allow it. The responses will only be used to check and improve the Handumanan system. 
3.4 System Development Methodology
The project adopts the Agile Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) framework and implements the Scrum framework. Scrum is being selected because it serves the researchers well, allowing them to develop the system step by step rather than building it all at once. It is appropriate for Handumanan as the system has certain features that require incremental iteration and refinement.

Phase 1: Requirement Analysis & Product Backlog Creation
During this phase, the researchers identified what the Handumanan system should be able to do based on its users' needs. The researchers considered the problems identified in the study, particularly the scattered, incomplete, and unreliable cultural heritage information available online. Based on these needs, the researchers identified the main features to be included in the system: allowing users to search for and browse heritage sites, view detailed site information, use location-based features, ask questions via the chatbot, create travel plans with the itinerary planner, and submit feedback. These tasks were then organized into a product backlog, which served as a guide for the development process.
Phase 2: Sprint Planning
During sprint planning, items from the product backlog were prioritized and divided into smaller development tasks. This helped the researchers work on the system step by step rather than develop all features at once. The researchers first focused on the system's core components, including the heritage site database, user authentication, search and browse functions, and admin content management. Other features, such as map integration, route directions, chatbot support, feedback, and itinerary planning, were also planned into the succeeding development tasks. Each feature was expected to be working, tested, and aligned with the study's objectives before being considered complete.
Phase 3: System Design & UI/UX Prototyping
This phase focused on designing the Handumanan system's look and functionality before actual development began. The researchers planned the website's layout, user action flow, and information presentation. The design included the system's main pages and modules, such as the homepage, heritage site list, detailed site information page, map and route section, chatbot interface, itinerary planner, feedback section, login and registration page, and admin management page. The design was prepared to make the system usable on both mobile phones and computers.
Phase 4: Iterative Development
During this phase, the Handumanan system was developed in steps using the Scrum approach. The researchers worked on smaller parts of the system rather than building everything at once. This helped the researchers focus on properly developing and testing each feature before moving to the next. The development included the user interface, heritage site database, search and browse functions, location-based features, review submission, admin heritage site record management, chatbot support, map and route features, and itinerary planning, which were tested.
Phase 5: Sprint Review and Testing
At the end of every sprint, the researchers checked the features that were already completed. This was done to see if each part of Handumanan was working correctly and matched the goals of the study. The tested features included user registration, search and browse, heritage site details, map and route directions, chatbot, feedback, admin management, and itinerary planning. If errors or problems were found, the researchers fixed them first before continuing to the next part of the system. 
Phase 6: Deployment
After the main features were completed and tested, the final version of Handumanan was prepared for presentation and use. Since the system is web-based, it can be accessed through a web browser using mobile phones and computers. This phase focused on ensuring the system was ready to demonstrate and that its main functions were working properly before being evaluated by selected users.
Phase 7: Maintenance
The maintenance phase is done after the system has been developed and tested. In this phase, the researchers will update heritage site information when there are new details or corrections. User feedback may also be checked to see what parts of the system can still be improved. If errors or bugs are found, they will be fixed to keep the system working properly. The admin access and user accounts will also be monitored for security, while the chatbot responses and itinerary suggestions may be updated when needed. 
3.5 Planning Phase
The planning phase was done before the actual coding of the Handumanan system. In this phase, the researchers identified the main problems in accessing cultural heritage site information in Metro Cebu and listed the system features needed to address them. These include user registration and login, search and browsing, detailed heritage site information, location-based suggestions, map and route directions, chatbot support, feedback, admin management, and the AI-powered itinerary planner. The researchers also organized the product backlog, planned the sprint activities, selected the needed tools, and prepared the system design before starting the development process. 
3.6 Analysis Phase
The analysis phase focused on identifying and organizing the requirements needed for the Handumanan system. In this phase, the researchers examined the needs of the target users and the main functions that the system should provide. This includes defining the user roles, functional requirements, non-functional requirements, and system diagrams such as the Use Case Diagram, Data Flow Diagram, and Class Diagram. This phase helped the researchers understand how users will interact with the system and how the system should process and manage information about cultural heritage sites.
3.6.1 User Requirements Specification (URS)
The system is designed to serve guests/visitors, registered users, and administrators. Tourism offices and LGUs are considered beneficiaries of the system because they may use the organized heritage site information for tourism promotion and cultural awareness. However, they do not have a separate system role unless they are given an administrator account.
Guests/Visitors: They can search, browse, and view information about heritage sites. They can also use location-based features, view routes, ask questions through the chatbot, and generate suggested itineraries.
Registered Users: They can access the same features as guests, but they can also save their favorite heritage sites, save itineraries, and submit reviews or ratings.
Administrators: They are responsible for managing heritage site records. They can add, edit, hide, or delete heritage site information and manage image URLs used in the system.
3.6.2 Functional Requirements
User Authentication: Guests can browse the system, while registered users can log in through Firebase Authentication to access features such as saving favorites, saving itineraries, and submitting reviews.   
Search and Browse Module: Users can search for heritage sites by name, category, or location. They can also browse heritage sites from organized lists.   
Detailed Site Information: Each heritage site page displays important details such as the site name, description, category, images, address, coordinates, location, and visiting information. 
Location-Based Features: The system uses browser geolocation, with user permission, to show nearby sites and support route planning.   
AI-Powered Itinerary Planner: The system can generate suggested travel plans based on the user’s location, available time, and preferences.   
Admin Module: Administrators can add, edit, hide/delete, and manage heritage site records. They can update site names, descriptions, categories, locations, coordinates, visiting information, status, and image URLs.  
Feedback System: Registered users can submit ratings and comments on heritage site pages. These reviews can be viewed on the related site page. 
Chatbot Feature: The system includes a chatbot that helps answer user questions about heritage sites, such as site details, location, and basic visiting information.

Figure 4.2: Class Diagram
Figure 4.2 shows the main classes and relationships used in the Handumanan system. The diagram includes the user account, heritage site records, reviews, favorites, itineraries, and service features such as chatbot, itinerary planning, and routing. 
The UserAuth class handles the user’s login and registration details, such as user ID, email, password hash, and account creation date. It is connected to UserProfile, which stores the user’s profile information, including display name, photo URL, role, and creation date. The AdminRole class is connected to the user profile and is used to identify if a user has administrator access.
The HeritageSite class stores the main information about each heritage site, such as site ID, name, description, category, location, coordinates, visiting information, status, image URLs, and date records. This class is connected to Review, Favorite, and ItineraryItem because users can review a specific heritage site, save it as a favorite, or include it in an itinerary.
The Review class stores ratings and comments submitted by registered users for a specific heritage site. The Favorite class stores the heritage sites saved by a user. The Itinerary class stores the user’s saved or generated travel plan, while ItineraryItem represents the specific heritage sites included in that itinerary.
The ChatbotService answers user questions using heritage site information as context, but it does not store chatbot records. The ItineraryPlanner generates itinerary suggestions based on user preferences, location, time, and heritage site data. The RoutingService supports route and direction features by using an external map or routing API. Overall, the class diagram shows how the main parts of Handumanan work together to support users, administrators, heritage site records, reviews, favorites, itineraries, chatbot assistance, and route planning.

3.7.2 Database Design
The database design for Handumanan was made to store and organize the records needed by the system. Firebase Authentication is used for user login and registration. Firebase Firestore stores user-related records and admin-managed records, while the system also uses built-in heritage site data bundled in the application as the default site directory. 
Database Collections:
/heritageSites – Stores admin-created or admin-updated heritage site records, including site name, description, category, location, coordinates, visiting information, status, and image URLs.
/heritageSites/{siteId}/reviews – Stores ratings and comments submitted by registered users for a specific heritage site.
/users/{userId}/favorites – Stores the heritage sites saved by a user as favorites.
/users/{userId}/itineraries – Stores the saved travel plans or generated itineraries of a user.
/roles_admin or user role field – Used to identify accounts with administrator access.
Built-in Heritage Site Data – Contains the default heritage site directory used by the application.

3.9 Development Phase
The development phase focused on building the Handumanan system in accordance with the planned requirements and design. 3.9.1 Technology Stack 
Frontend: Next.js, React, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS were used to develop the user interface, responsive layouts, navigation, map pages, admin dashboard, and interactive system components. 
Backend: Firebase was used as the backend service of the system. It connects the user interface to the database and supports system operations such as record management, user data handling, reviews, favorites, and saved itineraries. 
Database and Authentication: Firebase Firestore was used as the database for storing heritage site records, user profiles, reviews, favorites, and itineraries. Firebase Authentication was used for user login, registration, and admin access control. 
Maps and Location Services: Leaflet.js, OpenStreetMap, and the OpenRouteService API were used to display heritage site locations, show the user’s current location, suggest nearby sites, and generate route directions between selected destinations. 
AI Features: Genkit and Gemini were used to support the chatbot and itinerary planner. These features help users ask heritage-related questions, receive site suggestions, and generate personalized visit plans. 
IDEs & Tools: Antigravity was used as the development environment for building and managing the project.

Participants and Sampling
Purposive sampling will be used to select the study participants. This approach was selected due to the researchers' need to enlist persons who are close to tourism and cultural heritage, and who may be potential users of the system. These participants will be asked to provide constructive feedback on the platform's features, usability, and functionality. 
A total of sixty (60) will be involved in this study. These are divided into these groups: 
Tourism Office Personnel – Five (5) participants These participants can be from tourism offices or local government offices in Metro Cebu. They can provide feedback on cultural heritage, promote tourism, and disseminate information about the system's value to the public. 
Students – Twenty-five (25) participants Students are included because they are among the system's target users. They frequently use digital tools at school, in the learning process, and to learn about their local history. The feedback would be used to test the system for academic and general use. 
Local Residents – Twenty (20) participantsResidents from Metro Cebu are included because they are also potential users of the platform. They can provide feedback on the system's usefulness and relevance in real-world applications.  
Tourists / Travelers – Ten (10) participantsPeople who visit Metro Cebu or travel within Metro Cebu are involved in a test to see how much it helps them locate heritage sites, find directions for viewing, and plan visits. 
All selected participants will be asked to use the system and answer evaluation questionnaires. They will respond using the platform to gauge acceptability and performance. 
Setting of the Study
The study will be conducted in Metro Cebu, which comprises the cities of Cebu City, Lapu-Lapu City, Mandaue City, and Talisay City. The areas were selected because they are rich in cultural heritage places like museums, monuments, churches, and ancestral houses. 
Metro Cebu is also busy and urban, making it ideal for testing, implementing, and assessing a web-based cultural heritage information system for residents and visitors. The study was conducted in Metro Cebu because the area contains numerous historical and cultural heritage sites that require proper digital documentation and promotion, which is the spectrum of proposed system applications. The study in Metro Cebu enables researchers to collect relevant data and feedback from the target users, as it focuses on the protection of heritage locations in these cities. The system was designed primarily for Metro Cebu, but it can be adapted to create systems for other cities or provinces with cultural heritage sites.
