CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of the Study
Metro Cebu is rich in historical events that have shaped the city's identity. From its religious roots, from the first Spaniards who introduced Christianity, to the way Cebuanos live their day-to-day lives. These significant events have been commemorated through heritage sites such as churches, museums, monuments, and old structures. These sites play a crucial role not just in boosting tourism but, most notably, in the cultivation and preservation of our culture. Hence, these must be taken good care of and valued significantly.
Information about these cultural heritage sites can be easily accessed with a simple internet search, whether through a social media post, blog, website, or any digital platform. Thus, information systems enhance tourism promotion by allowing users to access platforms anytime, anywhere [5]. As studies show, when tourism information is well organized, visitors may have a positive experience and are more likely to visit additional places [1]. Hence, the Department of Tourism encourages the use of digital platforms to promote and raise public awareness of tourist spots [3]. 
 However, major concerns arise about the completeness, consistency,  and reliability of such information. Some details are vague or simply incorrect, reflecting personal interpretations of the cultural event and leading to misinformation. These lapses may result in both tourists and residents being misled or may not be fully aware of these heritage sites.
Another issue with such digital platforms is the lack of features that help individuals reach their destinations more quickly and conveniently, making it difficult for them to plan their visits. That said, platforms should be easy to use, with search functions and location-based services, as these are more user-friendly [6].
Aside from providing organized heritage information, the proposed system also supports users through location-based and AI-assisted features. Handumanan provides a searchable heritage directory, map-based route support, itinerary planning, visiting-hour awareness, and an AI chatbot that assists users with heritage-related questions. These features aim to make cultural heritage information more accessible to students, tourists, local residents, and heritage enthusiasts. 
To address these concerns, the researchers developed Handumanan: A Web-Based Cultural Heritage Site Information System for Metro Cebu, which aims to create a platform where users can easily access organized information about cultural heritage sites located particularly in the cities of Cebu, Lapu-lapu, Mandaue, and Talisay, and explore these sites more conveniently.
1.2 Statement of the Problem
Information about cultural heritage on the internet is often incomplete, inconsistent, and unreliable. This may mislead individuals who are not fully aware of such heritage, leading them to fail to explore it fully.  Also, digital platforms lack features that suggest places to visit, making it harder for individuals to explore nearby locations or plan their visits.
To address these concerns, this study seeks to answer the following questions:
How can cultural heritage site information in Metro Cebu be organized in a web-based system?
What system features can help users search, filter, and view heritage site information more efficiently?
How can map-based features support users in viewing site locations, routes, estimated distance, and estimated travel time?
How can an AI-powered itinerary planner assist users in creating suggested heritage travel plans?
How can a chatbot assist users in answering questions related to Metro Cebu heritage sites and the Handumanan system?
1.3 Objectives of the Study
1.3.1 General Objective:
To develop a web-based cultural heritage site information system for Metro Cebu that provides organized and accessible information for users.
1.3.2 Specific Objectives:
Design and develop a web-based system that provides organized information about selected heritage sites in Metro Cebu.
Provide search and filtering features based on city, category, and heritage site details.
Integrate map-based features that allow users to view heritage site location, generate routes, and access estimated distance and travel time.
Develop an AI-powered itinerary planning feature that recommends suggested heritage routes based on user preferences and available site information.
Implement an AI chatbot that answers questions related to Metro Cebu Heritage sites and the Handumanan system.
Provide an administrative module for managing heritage site records and maintaining site information
1.4 Significance of the Study
This study is significant because it contributes to the development of a cultural heritage platform. Specifically, this study will be beneficial to the following:
Tourists. The system helps tourists discover heritage sites, view site details, and plan routes using map-based guidance. It also assists them through itinerary planning and chatbot support. 
Tourism Offices and Local Government Units (LGUs). The system demonstrates how digital tools can support heritage promotion, information access, and cultural awareness. 
Community. The system may help promote local heritage awareness by making cultural heritage information easier to access. It can encourage residents to appreciate and learn more about heritage sites in Metro Cebu. .
Future Researchers. This study may serve as a basis for improving AI-assisted tourism systems, location-based services, and cultural heritage information systems. 
1.5 Scope and Limitations
This study focuses on developing a web-based cultural heritage site information system for selected heritage sites in Metro Cebu, particularly in Cebu City, Lapu-Lapu City, Mandaue City, and Talisay City. The system provides heritage site information such as descriptions, categories, images, coordinates, locations, and visiting information. Its main features include search and filtering, map-based site viewing, route generation, location-based suggestions, AI-powered itinerary planning, chatbot assistance, user reviews, and admin management of heritage site records. The system can be accessed through web browsers using mobile phones and computers.
However, the system is limited to the heritage sites encoded in the Handumanan directory and does not claim to include all heritage locations in Cebu. It also requires an internet connection to access the system and its online services. Map routes, estimated distance, and estimated travel time depend on the availability of the map service, user location permission, internet connection, and API limits. The chatbot is limited to Metro Cebu heritage tourism and Handumanan system related-questions. It does not answer unrelated topics and does not replace official tourism or historical authorities. The system is also web-based only and does not include a separate mobile application. Lastly, it does not provide real-time traffic monitoring, crowd monitoring, or visitor analytics.
1.6 Definition of Terms
To better understand the study, the following terms are defined based on how they are applied in this study:
Artificial Intelligence (AI). A system feature used to generate travel recommendations, suggest nearby heritage sites, and assist users in planning itineraries based on available site information and user preferences.
Backup Route Estimate. A fallback routing method used when Google route services are unavailable. It provides an approximate distance and travel time so users can still receive basic route guidance, although it is less accurate than Google-provided routing.
Chatbot. A built-in conversational assistance feature that helps users ask questions and receive information related to cultural heritage sites, directions, and travel recommendations within Metro Cebu.
Cultural Heritage Sites. These are locations in Metro Cebu with historical representation and cultural value, such as churches, museums, monuments, and old structures.
Database. An organized storage of system information. In Handumanan, Firebase Firestore stores user-related records, reviews, saved itineraries, favorites, and admin-managed heritage site records, and also uses built-in heritage site data. 
Feedback. A user review or comment submitted on a heritage site page to share ratings and opinions about the site. 
Firebase. A cloud-based platform used for authentication, database storage, and real-time data management in the system.
Geolocation. A function that detects the user's current location and is used to show nearby heritage sites.
Google Maps Integration. The use of Google Maps services to display heritage site locations, show the user’s current location, and support map-based interaction within the system.
Google Routes API. A Google Maps Platform service used by the system to generate route paths, estimated travel time, distance, and travel mode-based route information when available.
Information System. A system that collects, stores, and shows information to users in an organized way.
Itinerary. A planned list of places to visit, arranged in a proper order based on travel time and the time needed for each place.
Live Location Tracking. A system feature that updates the user’s current position on the map while navigation is active, subject to browser permission, device GPS accuracy, and internet connection.
Location-Based Recommendation. A system feature that suggests nearby heritage sites based on the user’s current geographic location.
Local Government Unit (LGU). These are the local authorities who are responsible for maintaining the inventory of heritage properties, preserving them, and implementing cultural programs within Metro Cebu. 
Navigation System. A feature that provides route guidance and directions from the user’s current location to selected cultural heritage sites using interactive maps and geolocation services.
Responsive Design. A design approach that allows the system interface to automatically adjust and display properly across different screen sizes and devices.
System administrator. The person with admin access who is responsible for managing heritage site records in the system, including adding, updating, hiding, or deleting site information, and managing image URLs. 
Tourism. This is an activity in which individuals visit places to explore, enjoy, and learn about other places’ tangible assets (e.g., tourist spots) and intangible assets such as culture and identity.
Travel Mode. A routing option that allows the user to request route estimates for driving, two-wheeler, transit, or walking when supported by the routing provider.
User Account. A registered account handled through Firebase Authentication that allows users to access features such as reviews, favorites, and saved itineraries. 
Web-Based System. A type of system that can be used through a web browser with an internet connection.

REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES
This chapter presents related literature and studies relevant to the development of Handumanan: A Web-Based Cultural Heritage Site Information Systems for Metro Cebu. 2.1 Related Literature
Cultural Heritage and Tourism
To understand how people are shaped today, it is important to remember history. One way to remember it is to preserve cultural heritage. Cultural heritage helps protect the identity, traditions, and historical experiences of a community. It also supports tourism because heritage sites allow visitors to learn about the past and experience local culture. According to Timothy and Boyd, heritage tourism helps people understand traditions and historical backgrounds through actual places and cultural experiences [1]. Cultural heritage tourism also helps strengthen cultural awareness and local identity [13]. Heritage sites help people remember important events, traditions, and ways of life from the past. By preserving and promoting these places through digital systems and tourism, younger people can learn more about local history and why it should be protected [1], [9], [13]. In addition, heritage tourism encourages cultural appreciation among both residents and visitors by allowing them to experience historical sites firsthand rather than only learning from books or media. To promote heritage tourism and encourage more visitors to explore cultural destinations, heritage information should be well organized. Hence, digital tools are important in preserving and promoting cultural heritage, making it more accessible to a wider audience [9]. Cultural heritage preservation is also supported by national and international frameworks that emphasize the importance of documenting, protecting, and promoting heritage resources for public awareness and sustainable tourism [10], [38], [39], [41], [42]. 
Digital Platforms in Tourism
In recent years, the tourism sector has changed because of technology. Many tourism businesses and organizations now use digital platforms to promote destinations and make tourism information easier to access. These platforms help users find details about tourist spots, maps, recommendations, and other travel-related information in one place. Because of this, users do not need to rely on many separate sources just to plan their visit. Online platforms also help increase visitor engagement by allowing tourism information to be shared and accessed more easily [7], [11]. Some tourism systems also use digital tools such as chatbots and recommendation features to assist users and improve their experience [21]. With the help of these systems, tourism services become more organized and convenient for both local and foreign visitors. Digital platforms also support tourism promotion by helping destinations become more visible to the public [30], [33]. In the Philippines, digital platforms are encouraged to improve accessibility and provide updated tourism content [3]. Related works also show that information technology, social media, and digital platforms continue to influence tourism promotion, destination visibility, and user access to travel-related information [34], [35], [43], [44], [45]. 
Web-Based Information Systems
An information system is used to collect, store, manage, and show data in an organized way. Web-based information systems are useful because users can open them through devices with an internet connection. In tourism and cultural heritage, this kind of system can help store important details such as site names, descriptions, locations, images, and visitor information. It also allows users to access tourism-related information whenever they need it [14]. These systems help manage organized data and support the development of useful platforms [15]. They also make it easier to store, find, and present information for users [16]. The development of web-based information systems also requires proper design principles so that the system can organize data, support user interaction, and provide useful information through an accessible platform [46]. 
Web-Based Cultural Heritage Information System
Web-based cultural heritage information systems help organize historical and cultural information in one digital platform. These systems allow users to search, browse, and view heritage site details without relying on scattered sources such as blogs, social media posts, or separate tourism pages. In cultural tourism, organized information helps users better understand the history, value, and location of heritage sites [1], [13]. For Handumanan, this concept is important because the system focuses on selected cultural heritage sites in Metro Cebu. The system provides site information such as historical background, images, categories, and location details. It also allows users to filter by city or category and access map-based support for easier exploration. This supports the purpose of making heritage information more accessible and organized for users [9], [11].
Usability and System Design
Usability is important in a web-based system because it affects how people use the platform. A system should have a clear layout, readable information, and proper navigation so users can find what they need without difficulty. Nielsen states that a system should be understandable and usable for its intended users [4]. Organized information also helps keep users interested while using the system [8]. A good interface can improve user interaction and satisfaction, especially when users need quick access to information [18]. In tourism systems, this is important because users often look for site details, maps, directions, and recommendations while planning their visit. For this reason, Handumanan should be designed to work properly on both mobile phones and computers [27]. Usability assessment and user satisfaction are important in web-based systems because the design, ease of use, and clarity of information affect how users experience the platform [47], [48].  
Location-Based Services and Google Maps Integration 
Location-based services allow users to find places and receive information based on their current location. In tourism systems, these services are useful because they help users discover nearby attractions, view possible routes, and plan their visits more conveniently. Geolocation technologies support navigation and decision-making, especially for users who are unfamiliar with the area [19]. Geographic information systems also help organize and present maps, locations, and spatial data in a more understandable way [20]. Google Maps integration is also useful in tourism-related systems because it helps display locations, route paths, estimated distance, and estimated travel time. Through map-based support, users can better understand where a destination is located and how they may travel from one place to another [28]. This is important in cultural heritage systems because users often need directions and route guidance when visiting heritage sites. In Handumanan, location-based services and Google Maps integration help users find nearby cultural heritage sites in Metro Cebu. The system can display heritage site locations, show the user’s current location when permission is granted, provide route guidance, and support itinerary planning by showing estimated distance and travel time. These features help users explore heritage sites more easily and plan their visits based on location, available time, and preferred destinations [6], [19], [28].
Artificial Intelligence in Tourism 
Artificial Intelligence is used in tourism systems to support recommendations, user assistance, and travel planning. Smart tourism studies show that technology can improve tourism experiences by making information more personalized and easier to access [21]. AI can help users by suggesting destinations, answering common questions, and supporting decision-making when planning visits [23]. In Handumanan, AI is applied through the chatbot and the AI-powered itinerary planner. The chatbot helps answer questions about heritage sites, such as history, location, and visitor information. The itinerary planner helps users generate suggested travel plans based on their location, available time, and preferences. These features support users in exploring Metro Cebu heritage sites more conveniently [21], [25].
AI Chatbots in Tourism and Information Systems
AI chatbots are used in tourism and information systems to help users ask questions and receive quick responses. Instead of manually searching through different pages, users can ask the chatbot in a conversational way. In tourism platforms, chatbots can help provide destination information, visitor guidance, and travel-related suggestions [21], [23]. In Handumanan, the chatbot serves as a heritage guide for Metro Cebu. It answers questions related to heritage site history, location, visiting information, nearby stops, and travel planning. To avoid unrelated or unsupported answers, the chatbot is limited to the scope of Handumanan and Metro Cebu heritage tourism.
Itinerary Planning and Recommender Systems
Itinerary planning systems help users organize places to visit based on time, location, and preferences. In tourism, recommender systems are useful because they help users choose destinations that match their interests and available travel time. These systems are helpful when users are unfamiliar with the place or when there are many possible destinations to visit [22], [25]. In Handumanan, the AI-powered itinerary planner recommends heritage sites based on the user’s location, selected preferences, and available time. It helps arrange suggested stops and supports route planning by showing estimated travel time and distance. This feature helps users create a more organized heritage visit instead of manually choosing and arranging sites from different sources [21], [25].
Web Technologies and System Development
Modern web technologies are important in developing interactive and responsive systems. These technologies help developers create websites that can display information, handle user actions, and connect to databases and external services. Web development tools support the creation of dynamic platforms that can run through web browsers [37]. Cloud-based services are also useful because they support online data storage and user authentication. Firebase supports web application development through authentication, database services, and cloud-based data management [52]. Google Maps Platform supports map display, site markers, user location, and route visualization, while Google Routes API supports route paths, distance estimates, estimated travel time, and travel mode-based routing when available [49], [50]. Gemini API and Genkit support the AI-related features of the system, including chatbot responses and AI-assisted itinerary planning [51], [53]. In Handumanan, these technologies help support heritage site browsing, user accounts, map-based route guidance, chatbot support, and itinerary planning [49], [50], [51], [52], [53]. 
Data Privacy in Location-Based Applications
Location-based applications should handle user location carefully because they may request access to the user’s current position. Users should be informed when location access is needed, and the system should only use location data for clear system functions. In systems that involve user interaction, proper privacy practices help protect users and support responsible system use [26], [27]. In Handumanan, location permission is requested only when needed for nearby site suggestions, route guidance, and itinerary planning. Users can still browse heritage sites, search the directory, view site details, and use other non-location features without sharing their location. This helps limit unnecessary data use while still allowing users to benefit from location-based features.

2.4 Research Gap

Some systems also lack integrated features such as AI-assisted itinerary planning, chatbot support, map-based route guidance, and user-friendly filtering by city or category. This creates a gap for a localized system that combines heritage information, location-based services, AI assistance, and travel planning in one platform [21], [25], [28]. Handumanan addresses this gap by providing a web-based cultural heritage site information system for selected sites in Cebu City, Lapu-Lapu City, Mandaue City, and Talisay City. The system combines a heritage site directory, map-based route guidance, location-based suggestions, AI-assisted itinerary planning, chatbot assistance, and admin-managed heritage records.
CHAPTER 3
METHODOLOGY
This chapter presents how the system was designed, developed, and tested for the study Handumanan: A Web-Based Cultural Heritage Site Information System for Metro Cebu. It includes the conceptual framework based on the Input-Process-Output (IPO) model, the research design, the system development process, the participants involved, and the methods used to collect and analyze data.
3.1 Conceptual Framework
The conceptual framework for Handumanan uses the Input-Process-Output (IPO) model. This type of model explains how the system works, detailing what information is fed into it, how it operates on that information, and what outputs result. It also provides insight into the user's interaction with the system and the flow of information in and out of the system.

Figure 1: IPO Model for Handumanan
3.1.2 Explanation of the Conceptual Framework
The IPO model serves as the foundation of how the Handumanan system operates:
Input: This is where users and admins enter all data into the system. Users put in their search queries, current location, preferences, questions for the chatbot, comments, ratings, etc. The system can also store information about heritage sites, including their names, descriptions, categories, images, locations, and visitor information. This information is added, updated, and managed by administrators to ensure that the system remains accurate and organized. 
Process: This stage demonstrates how the collected data is used. The system first organizes and stores the data in a Firebase database. It can then handle user operations such as searching, browsing, and filtering heritage sites. Location-based features recognize local locations, and an AI feature suggests locations and offers recommended tours. The system is also interfaced with map services to give directions and routes. User feedback is also collected and archived to aid system improvement. 
Output: This stage displays the system's output. Users can browse heritage sites, view detailed information, explore available suggestions, generate itineraries, and receive directions on maps. They may also inquire via the chatbot and leave comments after using the system. A dashboard provides administrators with management information and feedback from the site. These outputs enhance the user experience and promote heritage sites in Metro Cebu.  
3.2 Research Design
A Developmental Research Design is used in this study. This design was considered because the study's primary objective is to design and test a web-based system. For Handumanan, the researchers are not just identifying a problem; they also developed a solution through a cultural heritage site information system for Metro Cebu. 
3.3 Data Gathering Procedures
Data collection was conducted to identify the pain points of current methods for accessing information about cultural heritage sites. This involved: 
Review of Related Literature and Studies – The researchers reviewed books, journals, government reports, and online articles related to cultural heritage tourism and web-based systems. These were useful in pinpointing ideas and features that might be incorporated into the system.  
Online Research and Site Validation – The researchers gathered information from reliable online sources, including government sites, tourism pages, and trusted blogs for the heritage sites in Metro Cebu. Where possible, selected heritage sites were visited to validate their existence, collect images, and verify information.  
Interviews and Surveys – Informal interviews with the tourism officers, local residents, and possible users (students, tourists) were conducted. These assisted the researchers in gaining insight into their needs and the issues that they encounter when searching for information about heritage sites. A brief online survey was also distributed to gather user opinions on features they found useful, such as search tools, maps, itinerary planning, and the chatbot. 
Observation – Observation was used to see how people usually search for heritage information on websites and social media. This helped the researchers notice common problems, such as outdated information and the lack of location-based features. 
3.3.1 Ethical Considerations
In compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173), the researchers adhered to ethical practices when developing and testing the Handumanan system. Since the system involves user accounts, location-based features, feedback, and information about cultural heritage sites, the researchers ensured that the rights and privacy of all participants and users are protected.
Informed Consent
Before the participants use the system or answer the questionnaire, the researchers explained what the study is about. They were told that joining the study was their choice  and that their answers will only be used for school/research purposes. The researchers also informed them  about the data that may be collected, such as their ratings, comments, and basic account information if they register in the system.
User Privacy and Data Protection
Any information received by the system was collected only when required for its functions, namely user account details, feedback, saved itineraries, and location input (only in case the location-based feature is used). The information was not shared with anyone else or used for anything other than the study. To ensure data security, only authorized users will have access to the admin machine. 
Location Data Privacy
Handumanan also comes with location-oriented capabilities: Users were informed  that their location data could be processed to recommend nearby heritage sites and generate travel routes. Only the user's location was used for this purpose. Users can grant or deny permission for location access based on their preference. 
Accuracy of Cultural Heritage Information
The researchers ensured  that information about the cultural heritage site added to the system is sourced from trusted sources such as government websites and tourist offices. This is critical to not provide false or misleading information. The researchers will carefully examine the information before adding it to the system. 
Proper Use of Images and Content
Images and content for the system will be sourced from existing sources or, where possible, collected by the researchers. The researchers will not use any copyrighted materials without permission or citation. All information related to the heritage sites will also be presented well, as these are now part of Metro Cebu's history and culture. 
Confidentiality of Feedback and Survey Responses
The answers from surveys, interviews, and online feedback forms were kept private. The names and personal information of the respondents will not be shown in the final paper unless they allow it. The responses will only be used to check and improve the Handumanan system. 
3.4 System Development Methodology
The project adopts the Agile Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) framework and implements the Scrum framework. Scrum was selected because it serves the researchers well, allowing them to develop the system step by step rather than building it all at once. It is appropriate for Handumanan as the system has certain features that require incremental iteration and refinement.
Figure 2: Agile Model for Handumanan
Phase 1: Requirement Analysis & Product Backlog Creation
During this phase, the researchers identified what the Handumanan system should be able to do based on its users' needs. The researchers considered the problems identified in the study, particularly the scattered, incomplete, and unreliable cultural heritage information available online. Based on these needs, the researchers identified the main features to be included in the system: allowing users to search for and browse heritage sites, view detailed site information, use location-based features, ask questions via the chatbot, create travel plans with the itinerary planner, and submit feedback. These tasks were then organized into a product backlog, which served as a guide for the development process.
Phase 2: Sprint Planning
During sprint planning, items from the product backlog were prioritized and divided into smaller development tasks. This helped the researchers work on the system step by step rather than develop all features at once. The researchers first focused on the system's core components, including the heritage site database, user authentication, search and browse functions, and admin content management. Other features, such as map integration, route directions, chatbot support, feedback, and itinerary planning, were also planned into the succeeding development tasks. Each feature was expected to be working, tested, and aligned with the study's objectives before being considered complete.
Phase 3: System Design & UI/UX Prototyping
This phase focused on designing the Handumanan system's look and functionality before actual development began. The researchers planned the website's layout, user action flow, and information presentation. The design included the system's main pages and modules, such as the homepage, heritage site list, detailed site information page, map and route section, chatbot interface, itinerary planner, feedback section, login and registration page, and admin management page. The design was prepared to make the system usable on both mobile phones and computers.
Phase 4: Iterative Development
During this phase, the Handumanan system was developed in steps using the Scrum approach. The researchers worked on smaller parts of the system rather than building everything at once. This helped the researchers focus on properly developing and testing each feature before moving to the next. The development included the user interface, heritage site database, search and browse functions, location-based features, review submission, admin heritage site record management, chatbot support, map and route features, and itinerary planning, which were tested.
Phase 5: Sprint Review and Testing
At the end of every sprint, the researchers checked the features that were already completed. This was done to see if each part of Handumanan was working correctly and matched the goals of the study. The tested features included user registration, search and browse, heritage site details, map and route directions, chatbot, feedback, admin management, and itinerary planning. If errors or problems were found, the researchers fixed them first before continuing to the next part of the system. 
Phase 6: Deployment
After the main features were completed and tested, the final version of Handumanan was prepared for presentation and use. Since the system is web-based, it can be accessed through a web browser using mobile phones and computers. This phase focused on ensuring the system was ready to demonstrate and that its main functions were working properly before being evaluated by selected users.
Phase 7: Maintenance
During the maintenance phase, heritage site information may be updated when new details or corrections are available. User feedback may also be checked to identify parts of the system that can still be improved. If errors or bugs are found, they may be fixed to keep the system working properly.

Figure 5: Class Diagram
Figure 5 shows the main classes and relationships used in the Handumanan system. The diagram includes the user account, heritage site records, reviews, favorites, itineraries, and service features such as chatbot, itinerary planning, and routing. 
The UserAuth class handles the user’s login and registration details, such as user ID, email, password hash, and account creation date. It is connected to UserProfile, which stores the user’s profile information, including display name, photo URL, role, and creation date. The AdminRole class is connected to the user profile and is used to identify if a user has administrator access.
The HeritageSite class stores the main information about each heritage site, such as site ID, name, description, category, location, coordinates, visiting information, status, image URLs, and date records. This class is connected to Review, Favorite, and ItineraryItem because users can review a specific heritage site, save it as a favorite, or include it in an itinerary.
The Review class stores ratings and comments submitted by registered users for a specific heritage site. The Favorite class stores the heritage sites saved by a user. The Itinerary class stores the user’s saved or generated travel plan, while ItineraryItem represents the specific heritage sites included in that itinerary.
The ChatbotService answers user questions using heritage site information as context, but it does not store chatbot records. The ItineraryPlanner generates itinerary suggestions based on user preferences, location, time, and heritage site data. The RoutingService supports route and direction features by using an external map or routing API. Overall, the class diagram shows how the main parts of Handumanan work together to support users, administrators, heritage site records, reviews, favorites, itineraries, chatbot assistance, and route planning.

3.7.2 Database Design
The database design for Handumanan was made to store and organize the records needed by the system. Firebase Authentication is used for user login and registration. Firebase Firestore stores user-related records and admin-managed records, while the system also uses built-in heritage site data bundled in the application as the default site directory. 
Database Collections:
/heritageSites – Stores admin-created or admin-updated heritage site records, including site name, description, category, location, coordinates, visiting information, status, and image URLs.
/heritageSites/{siteId}/reviews – Stores ratings and comments submitted by registered users for a specific heritage site.
/users/{userId}/favorites – Stores the heritage sites saved by a user as favorites.
/users/{userId}/itineraries – Stores the saved travel plans or generated itineraries of a user.
/roles_admin or user role field – Used to identify accounts with administrator access.
Built-in Heritage Site Data – Contains the default heritage site directory used by the application.

Setting of the Study
The study was conducted in MetroCebu, which comprises the cities of Cebu City, Lapu-Lapu City, Mandaue City, and Talisay City. The areas were selected because they are rich in cultural heritage places like museums, monuments, churches, and ancestral houses. 
Metro Cebu is also busy and urban, making it ideal for testing, implementing, and assessing a web-based cultural heritage information system for residents and visitors. The study was conducted in Metro Cebu because the area contains numerous historical and cultural heritage sites that require proper digital documentation and promotion, which is the spectrum of proposed system applications. The study in Metro Cebu enables researchers to collect relevant data and feedback from the target users, as it focuses on the protection of heritage locations in these cities. The system was designed primarily for Metro Cebu, but it can be adapted to create systems for other cities or provinces with cultural heritage sites.
